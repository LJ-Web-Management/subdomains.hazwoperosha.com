Spill response

The 7 basic steps

Procedures vary by facility, but spill response generally follows this sequence:

  1. Identify

    The substance, hazards, size and potential risks.

  2. Notify

    Nearby personnel and supervisors as required.

  3. Protect

    Select protection based on the hazard assessment.

  4. Contain

    Keep it from spreading to drains, soil or other areas.

  5. Clean up

    Use proper absorbents and approved methods.

  6. Dispose

    Manage contaminated materials under hazardous waste rules.

  7. Report

    Complete required internal and regulatory reporting.

Assess first

Significant or non-significant?

Non-significant

A small release that trained employees can safely manage with available spill kits and workplace procedures. Usually limited health or environmental risk.

Significant

Greater hazards: toxic exposure, fire risk, large chemical releases or conditions that need outside emergency responders.

FAQ

Common questions

What are the seven basic steps of spill response?

Procedures vary by facility, but spill response generally follows seven steps: identify the substance, hazards, size and risks; notify nearby personnel and supervisors; select protection based on the hazard assessment; prevent the spill from spreading to drains, soil or other areas; clean up with proper absorbents and approved methods; manage contaminated materials under hazardous waste rules; and complete required internal and regulatory reporting.

What is the difference between a significant and a non-significant spill?

A non-significant spill is a small release trained employees can safely manage with available spill kits and workplace procedures. A significant spill involves greater hazards, such as toxic exposure, fire risk or a large chemical release, and may require outside emergency responders.

How do California spill reporting requirements differ from federal requirements?

California rules may require additional notifications and faster timelines than federal rules, and some spills must be reported to state agencies even below federal thresholds. Depending on the incident, employers may need to contact Cal OES, Cal/EPA, local environmental agencies or emergency responders, and federal reporting to the EPA or National Response Center may also apply.

What is Title 8, CCR Section 5192?

Section 5192 is California's HAZWOPER regulation, enforced by Cal/OSHA and closely aligned with federal OSHA HAZWOPER. It covers employee training, medical surveillance, PPE, emergency response planning and site safety for hazardous substance operations.

Is spill prevention training mandatory?

OSHA does not name spill training as a standalone requirement, but where hazardous chemicals are used or stored, employers must train employees to respond safely to spills under standards such as HAZWOPER (29 CFR 1910.120) and Hazard Communication (29 CFR 1910.1200).
