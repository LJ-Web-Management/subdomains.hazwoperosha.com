Safety information: OSHA vs. NFPA 70E

OSHA is the law. Its electrical standards (29 CFR 1910 Subpart S and 29 CFR 1926 Subpart K) say what must be achieved. NFPA 70E is the consensus standard that explains how: hazard assessments, protection boundaries, arc-rated PPE and equipment labeling. OSHA looks to NFPA 70E to show how its requirements can be met, and this course follows the 2027 edition.

Questions

What is an arc flash?

An arc flash happens when electric current leaves its intended path and travels through the air from one conductor to another, or to ground. It releases intense heat and light that can cause serious burns or death.

What is the relationship between OSHA and NFPA 70E?

OSHA is the law. Its electrical standards are performance-based, and OSHA looks to NFPA 70E, a consensus standard for electrical safety-related work practices, for the prescriptive practices that show how to meet those requirements.

Which regulations does this arc flash course cover?

OSHA 29 CFR 1910 Subpart S (general industry), 29 CFR 1926 Subpart K (construction) and the 2027 edition of NFPA 70E.

What is an arc flash study?

An arc flash study is an engineering analysis of a facility's electrical system. It calculates incident energy, sets protection boundaries, and identifies the PPE and labels needed for work on or near energized equipment.
