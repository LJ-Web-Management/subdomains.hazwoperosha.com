OSHA vs. NFPA 70E

OSHA's electrical standards are the law and are performance-based. NFPA 70E is the consensus standard that spells out the work practices OSHA looks to for meeting them. Following 70E is usually not itself legally required, but it is the industry-recognized way to protect workers.

FAQ

How do OSHA and NFPA 70E relate?

OSHA's electrical standards are performance-based and are the law. NFPA 70E is the consensus standard that sets out the prescriptive work practices OSHA looks to for meeting those requirements.

Is NFPA 70E mandatory?

In most cases following NFPA 70E is not itself a legal requirement. However, OSHA requires employers to protect employees from electrical hazards, and NFPA 70E is the industry-recognized way to do that.

What does the employer do after training?

Under NFPA 70E, the employer audits its electrical safety program at least every three years to verify that employees follow the required safety-related work practices.
