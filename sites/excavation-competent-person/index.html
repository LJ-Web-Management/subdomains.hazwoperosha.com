FAQ

Who is a Competent Person for excavations?

Someone designated by the employer who can identify existing and predictable hazards and is authorized to take prompt corrective measures to eliminate them. Under the excavation standard, the competent person's tasks include classifying soil, inspecting protective systems, designing structural ramps, monitoring water removal equipment and conducting site inspections.

Does OSHA require a Competent Person on excavation sites?

Yes. OSHA's excavation standard, 29 CFR 1926 Subpart P, relies on a competent person to inspect excavations, adjacent areas and protective systems. This course provides training for people their employer designates in that role.

Are there prerequisites?

No. There are no prerequisites. The course takes about 8 hours and is available in English and Spanish.

How long is the certificate valid?

You can download your certificate as soon as you pass the final exam. It is valid for two years; retake the course within 24 months to stay current.

Does it cover digging near live electrical lines?

The course covers locating and working near underground utilities. Work near live electrical lines may call for additional electrical safety training, such as the arc flash safety course.
