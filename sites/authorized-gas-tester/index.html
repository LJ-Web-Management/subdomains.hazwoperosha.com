FAQ

What does an Authorized Gas Tester do?

An Authorized Gas Tester (AGT) tests the atmosphere before and during high-risk work such as confined space entry and hot work, interprets the readings and confirms conditions are safe under the permit-to-work system. Many hazardous gases are invisible and odorless, so they can only be detected with the right equipment used correctly.

Which OSHA standards does the AGT course align with?

29 CFR 1910.146 (permit-required confined spaces), 1910.120 (HAZWOPER), 1910.147 (lockout/tagout), 1910.252 (welding, cutting and brazing), 1910.106 (flammable liquids), 1910.1000 (air contaminants), 1910.134 (respiratory protection), 1910 Subpart Z (toxic and hazardous substances), 1910.119 (process safety management) and the OSH Act General Duty Clause, Section 5(a)(1).

How does AGT training support the permit-to-work system?

Trained AGTs perform accurate atmospheric evaluations that confirm a work area is safe before the task starts, and they understand how gas testing ties into permits, isolation (lockout/tagout) and operational controls.

How long is the course and certificate?

The course takes about 8 hours across 7 modules and 19 lessons, followed by a final exam. The certificate of completion is valid for 12 months.
