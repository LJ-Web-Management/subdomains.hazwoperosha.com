Why it matters

Asbestos was prized for centuries because it resists heat and fire, and it ended up in insulation, flooring, roofing and countless building products. Left intact it is mostly harmless. Once it is drilled, cut, sanded or crumbled, microscopic fibers become airborne. Inhaled, they can cause asbestosis, lung cancer and mesothelioma, often decades after exposure.

OSHA requires asbestos training when employee exposure meets or exceeds the permissible exposure limit (PEL). This course gives the people most likely to stumble on asbestos-containing materials (ACM) during routine work the knowledge to recognize them and stop before they disturb them.

This is an awareness course. It does not qualify anyone to remove or abate asbestos. Class I–IV work requires additional training.

Readers ask

Which OSHA standards does the asbestos awareness course cover?

OSHA's asbestos standard for general industry, 29 CFR 1910.1001, and for construction, 29 CFR 1926.1101.

Does asbestos awareness training qualify me to remove asbestos?

No. This awareness course is for workers who may accidentally disturb asbestos-containing materials. It does not qualify anyone to perform asbestos removal or abatement; employers must provide additional training for Class I–IV asbestos work.

Who needs asbestos awareness training?

Maintenance and custodial staff, electricians, plumbers, HVAC technicians, renovation and remodeling crews, facility managers and building engineers, and school, hospital and property maintenance personnel who work in buildings that may contain asbestos.

How long is the certificate valid?

One year. You can download your PDF certificate immediately after passing the final exam and renew by taking the course again after 12 months.

Are there any prerequisites?

No. There are no prerequisites for the OSHA Asbestos Awareness Training course.
