Three tanks, three ways to handle

Lesson 2 shows how tank design affects liquid surge and handling.

Baffled

Bulkheads with openings let liquid flow through while slowing front-to-back surge. Side-to-side surge can still occur.

Bulkhead

Solid dividers split the tank into separate compartments. Weight distribution depends on how each compartment is loaded.

Smooth-bore

No internal walls, so nothing slows the liquid. Common for food-grade loads such as milk that must be kept clean.

FAQ

When do I need a Tanker (N) endorsement?

When the vehicle you drive meets the federal definition of a tank vehicle: any single tank of 119 gallons or more, or multiple tanks with a combined capacity of 1,000 gallons or more, permanently or temporarily attached. Drivers hauling large liquid totes or IBCs on flatbeds or in dry vans may meet this definition too.

Does FMCSA require reported ELDT for the N endorsement?

No. FMCSA requires reported entry-level driver training for Class A, B and C licenses and for the H, P and S endorsements, not for N. For the N endorsement you pass the tank vehicle knowledge test at your state licensing agency. This course prepares you for that test.

What do I need to get the N endorsement?

A CDL or CDL permit for the appropriate class, a passing score on the state tank vehicle knowledge test, and compliance with federal and state age, medical and eligibility standards. The endorsement is renewed through your state's CDL renewal process.

What about hazmat in tanks?

Hauling hazardous liquids or gases in a tank requires the X endorsement, which combines the Hazmat (H) and Tanker (N) endorsements.
