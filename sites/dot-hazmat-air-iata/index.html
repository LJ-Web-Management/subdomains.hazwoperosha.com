Passenger aircraft or cargo only?

Quantity limits depend on where the cargo flies. The Hazardous Materials Table lists them in two columns.

Column 9A

Passenger aircraft or rail

Maximum net quantity per package when passengers are on board.

Column 9B

Cargo aircraft only

Often allows larger quantities, but some materials can only fly on cargo aircraft.

FAQ

Which regulations does the course cover?

The DOT Hazardous Materials Regulations in 49 CFR Part 175 (Carriage by Aircraft), with applicable IATA requirements. DOT hazmat training requirements themselves are in 49 CFR Part 172 Subpart H.

How often must I retrain?

DOT requires recurrent hazmat training at least once every three years. The certificate is valid for three years; retake the full course before it expires.

Are there prerequisites?

No. It builds on the 4-hour DOT Hazmat Transportation General Awareness course, but there are no prerequisites. The course is available in English and Spanish.

Who should take it?

Air carriers, indirect air carriers and freight forwarders; anyone who packages, marks, labels, loads or unloads hazmat for passenger or cargo aircraft; flight crew; air cargo security and inspection staff; and compliance officers.
