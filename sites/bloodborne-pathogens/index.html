FAQ

What are bloodborne pathogens?

Bloodborne pathogens are infectious microorganisms in human blood that can cause disease, including hepatitis B virus (HBV), hepatitis C virus (HCV) and human immunodeficiency virus (HIV). They spread through blood and other potentially infectious materials (OPIM).

Does bloodborne pathogens training have to be taken every year?

Yes. Under OSHA's Bloodborne Pathogens Standard, 29 CFR 1910.1030, employees with occupational exposure to blood or OPIM must receive this training annually. The course certificate is valid for one year.

What did the Needlestick Safety and Prevention Act change?

The Needlestick Safety and Prevention Act of 2000 modified the standard to require employers to identify, evaluate and implement safer medical devices, keep a sharps injury log, and involve non-managerial healthcare workers in choosing engineering and work practice controls.

Are there any prerequisites?

No. There are no prerequisites for the OSHA Bloodborne Pathogens Training course.
