The four types of workplace violence

The course covers all four legally defined types with realistic scenarios.

Type 1

No legitimate business

Violence by someone with no legitimate business at the worksite, such as a robbery.

Type 2

Customers & the public

Violence directed at employees by customers, clients, patients, students or visitors. It is one of the most commonly reported categories.

Type 3

Co-workers

Violence by a current or former co-worker, supervisor or manager.

Type 4

Personal relationships

Violence by someone with a personal relationship to an employee, such as domestic violence that follows them to work.

Three lessons

  1. Understanding workplace violence

    Its forms and modes, the four types, warning signs, and the effects on employees, employers and operations.

  2. Prevention, response & emergency preparedness

    Preventing and safely responding to aggressive behavior, emergency action plans, incident reporting and investigation, and post-incident follow-up.

  3. Workplace violence prevention program

    Assessments and security reviews, hazard identification and risk control, policy, responsibilities, employee participation, job hazard analysis, training, recordkeeping and program evaluation.

Who should take it

Any California employee, supervisor or manager whose employer is covered by SB 553, including staff in retail, hospitality, warehousing, education and offices. It is especially useful for HR and safety staff who build the plan, and for anyone who deals directly with customers or the public.

Your employer still has a part to play

A WVPP can't be generic. Alongside this course, your employer must walk you through your worksite's own written plan, its emergency procedures, and where to find the Violent Incident Log.

Who is exempt?

  • Certain law enforcement agencies

Questions

What are the four types of workplace violence?

Type 1: violence by someone with no legitimate business at the worksite, such as a robbery. Type 2: violence against employees by customers, clients, patients, students or visitors. Type 3: violence by a current or former co-worker, supervisor or manager. Type 4: violence by someone with a personal relationship to an employee, such as domestic violence that follows them to work.

Who must have a Workplace Violence Prevention Plan in California?

Since July 1, 2024, nearly every California employer, regardless of size or industry, must establish, implement and maintain a written Workplace Violence Prevention Plan (WVPP), train employees on it, and keep a Violent Incident Log for five years.

Is my business exempt from the WVPP requirement?

Very few employers are. The main exemptions are healthcare facilities covered by Title 8 Section 3342, facilities operated by the Department of Corrections and Rehabilitation, certain law enforcement agencies, employees teleworking from a location of their own choosing, and worksites with fewer than 10 employees that have no public access and already comply with IIPP requirements. Employers must be able to show an exemption applies.

Does this course fulfill my annual WVPP training requirement?

It covers the core content SB 553 requires for initial and annual training. Because a WVPP must address the hazards of each work area, your employer still needs to walk you through your worksite's written plan, its emergency procedures, and where to find the Violent Incident Log.
