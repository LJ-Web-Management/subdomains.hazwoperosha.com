Fight or flee?

Fighting a fire is only appropriate in the incipient stage: small, confined to its point of origin, and controllable with a portable extinguisher or small hose. Anything beyond that, evacuate.

Annual training

Employers who provide portable extinguishers must educate employees at initial employment and at least annually. Everyone must be briefed on the Emergency Action Plan and relevant parts of the Fire Prevention Plan, and designated firefighting staff need additional equipment training.

FAQ

Do I need to fight every fire I see?

No. Fighting a fire is only appropriate in the incipient stage, when it is small, confined to its point of origin and controllable with a portable extinguisher or small hose. Beyond that, the right move is to evacuate.

How often is fire extinguisher training required in California?

Employers who provide portable fire extinguishers for employee use must educate employees upon initial employment and at least annually. Every employee must be briefed on the Emergency Action Plan and the parts of the Fire Prevention Plan they need to know, and employees designated to use firefighting equipment need additional equipment training.

Which Cal/OSHA standards does the course cover?

Title 8 CCR Section 6151 (Portable Fire Extinguishers), Section 3220 (Emergency Action Plan), Section 3221 (Fire Prevention Plan), the means of egress requirements in Sections 3215 through 3235, and the fire protection requirements in Group 27 (Sections 6150 through 6184).

Does this apply to construction sites?

The course is based on Cal/OSHA's General Industry Safety Orders. Construction sites also fall under the Construction Safety Orders, which have their own fire protection requirements, so construction employers should confirm which orders apply. The fire safety, extinguisher and evacuation principles still apply.
