Is it a confined space?

OSHA uses three criteria. A space that meets all three is a confined space.

  1. 1

    Large enough and configured so a worker can fully enter it

  2. 2

    Has limited or restricted means of entry or exit

  3. 3

    Is not designed for continuous occupancy

When does it need a permit?

A confined space becomes permit-required when it has one or more of these four conditions (29 CFR 1926.1202).

Contains or may contain a hazardous atmosphere

Contains material that could engulf an entrant

Has an internal shape that could trap or asphyxiate an entrant, such as inwardly converging walls

Contains any other serious safety or health hazard

FAQ

What is a confined space?

Under OSHA, a space is confined if it is large enough and configured so a worker can fully enter it, has limited or restricted means of entry or exit, and is not designed for continuous occupancy. Examples include tanks, ducts, crawl spaces, manholes, attics, turbines and drilled shafts.

Does this course qualify me to enter confined spaces?

No. This is awareness-level training only and does not qualify employees to enter confined spaces. Entrants, attendants and entry supervisors need training specific to their role and the spaces they work in.

Are there prerequisites?
