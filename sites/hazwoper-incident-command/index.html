Who this is for

OSHA requires on-scene incident commanders who take control of a hazardous substance release to have training beyond the operations level, as set out in 29 CFR 1910.120(q)(6)(v). That includes knowing how to implement the employer's incident command system and emergency response plan, and understanding the hazards of working in chemical protective clothing.

  • Personnel designated as on-scene incident commanders
  • Personnel who lead or oversee incident command operations
  • Leaders and supervisors of HAZMAT teams
  • Managers of a facility or site emergency response plan
  • Anyone coordinating an Incident Command System (ICS)

Questions

Which OSHA regulations does the 8-hour Incident Command course cover?

The course meets the requirements of OSHA 29 CFR 1910.120(q) and 29 CFR 1926.65(q), including the on-scene incident commander training described in 29 CFR 1910.120(q)(6)(v).

How long does the course take?

About 8 hours. It has 5 modules and 55 lessons, taken in order, followed by a final exam. You have 3 months to finish and 1 year of access to the materials.

How do I renew my Incident Command certificate?

OSHA requires on-site incident commanders to complete 8 hours of annual refresher training. The certificate is valid for one year, and you renew it by retaking this same 8-hour course.

When do I get my certificate?

Immediately after you pass the final exam, you can download, save or print your Course Completion Certificate as a PDF.
