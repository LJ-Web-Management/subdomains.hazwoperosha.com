Compressed Gas Cylinder Safety

A cylinder's stored pressure can mean explosion, fire, toxic exposure or oxygen displacement when equipment fails, a connection is wrong or gas escapes. This 2-hour Cal/OSHA course teaches welders, maintenance techs and industrial workers to inspect, store, move and use cylinders safely.

Five lessons

  1. Intro to cylinders

    Definitions, components, gas types and classifications, cylinder construction, and the Cal/OSHA sections that apply.

  2. Common hazards

    Stored-pressure release, fire and explosion, hazard communication, asphyxiation and poisoning, incompatibility, and the dangers of PVC/CPVC pipe in gas systems.

  3. Inspection & installation

    Labels, markings and test dates; valves, caps and relief devices; regulator compatibility; safe installation; abnormal pressure signs.

  4. Storage & transportation

    Ventilation and heat protection, oxygen and fuel-gas separation, special gases, mechanical handling aids, carts and crane moves, and preventing tip-overs.

  5. Safe work practices

    Valves and regulators, welding and cutting with flashback arresters, PPE, leak detection, emergency response, unidentified cylinders, and a chlorine-exposure case study.

FAQ

Which Cal/OSHA standards does the gas cylinder course cover?

Title 8 Section 4650 (storage, handling and use of cylinders), 4649 (construction and marking of cylinders), 4657 (safety relief devices), 1740 (storage and use of cylinders in construction), 3203 (Injury and Illness Prevention Program) and 5194 (Hazard Communication). It also touches on Sections 4838, 4845, 5157 and 5144.

Is refresher training required?

There is no universally mandated annual refresher. Retaking the course every 36 months is recommended, and employees may need extra training when they take on new responsibilities or workplace hazards change. The certificate is valid for three years.

Who should take compressed gas cylinder safety training?

Welders, cutting operators and fabricators using fuel-gas cylinders such as acetylene and oxygen; foremen, safety coordinators and supervisors; workers who receive, inspect, secure or prepare cylinders; and maintenance staff and technicians who attach regulators or investigate leaks.

Are there any prerequisites?

No. There are no prerequisites for the Cal/OSHA Compressed Gas Cylinder Safety Training course.
