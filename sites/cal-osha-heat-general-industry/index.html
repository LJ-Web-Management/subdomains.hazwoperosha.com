FAQ

What temperatures trigger Cal/OSHA heat illness requirements?

Outdoors, shade must be available when temperatures exceed 80°F, and high-heat procedures begin at 95°F for certain industries such as agriculture and construction. Indoors, the standard applies when temperatures reach 82°F while employees are present; additional assessment and control measures apply at 87°F, or at 82°F for workers in restrictive clothing or high radiant heat areas.

Does this online course meet all of my Cal/OSHA heat illness training requirements?

It covers the general training topics required by Sections 3395 and 3396. Cal/OSHA also requires training on each employer's own heat illness procedures, so employers should add site-specific details such as water and cool-down locations, emergency contacts and reporting steps.

Is a written Heat Illness Prevention Plan required?

Yes. Both the outdoor (§3395) and indoor (§3396) standards require a written Heat Illness Prevention Plan.

How often should heat illness training be repeated?

The Cal/OSHA standards do not set a specific retraining interval, but training is required before employees begin work with heat illness risk. Many employers retrain every year before summer, and when procedures, duties or worksite conditions change. The course certificate is valid for one year.
